# NMAMIT Internship Guidelines (summary)

This is a summary of the internship rules for B.Tech students of NMAM Institute of Technology (NMAMIT), Nitte,
compiled from the B.Tech CSE Regulations & Curriculum 2023 (students admitted from 2023-24).
Page numbers are PDF page numbers. Students should confirm details with their department internship coordinator.

## Overview of mandatory internships

Internship is a University Core Course (UCC) and is mandatory for the award of the B.Tech degree.
Every B.Tech student must complete Internship-I and Internship-II, a total of 10 weeks, earning 10 credits.
Internships are done during the vacations at the end of the 1st, 2nd and 3rd academic years and are evaluated in
the IV and VIII semesters. Students who do not take up or complete the internship are declared failed in that
course and must complete it in later university examinations after meeting the internship requirements.
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, sections 8.2 and 17.3.3, PDF pages 43-44)

The curriculum gives 8-12 credits to Internship (INT) under University Core Courses (10 credits in the CSE scheme).
Earning 10 credits through Internship/Training/Specialised courses is mandatory before any qualification is
awarded at an exit point. (Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, sections 3.1 and 8.2)

## Internship-I (UC1001-1) - Activity based / Intra-institutional internship

### Duration, timing and credits of Internship-I
- Duration: 2 weeks (80-90 hours).
- When: during the vacations of the I and II semesters (first year).
- Type: intra/inter-institutional, activity-based internship.
- Lateral entry students (admitted to III semester) do Internship-I during the 3rd semester or in the break between the 3rd and 4th semesters.
- Credits: 2. The credit appears on the IV semester grade card.

(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, scheme tables and section 11.5(ii), PDF pages 24, 26 and 33)

### How is Internship-I evaluated?
- Internship-I is evaluated for 100 marks, CIE only (no SEE).
- Marks: 25 marks for the first presentation, 25 marks for the second presentation, and 50 marks for the report and final presentation.
- Students submit the report to their mentor as soon as the internship ends. The mentor conducts the examination.
- A viva-voce (presentation followed by a question-answer session) is held during the I/II/III semester.
- The faculty coordinator or mentor monitors the student's progress and guides them to complete the internship.

(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, section 11.5(ii), PDF page 33)

### Activities that count towards Internship-I
Activities that count towards Internship-I include: inter/intra-institutional workshops or training, work on a
consultancy or research project, technical/business festival events, contributing to the Incubation/Innovation/
Entrepreneurship Cell, learning at a departmental lab, tinkering lab or institutional workshop, and other similar
activities. Each activity is graded Excellent (80-100), Good (60-79), Satisfactory (40-59) or Unsatisfactory/fail
(below 40). The evidence is the student's diary and the internship report with a certificate from the relevant
authority. The work is evaluated by the institute faculty mentor, together with an external expert if there is one.
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, Internship-I assessment rubrics table, PDF pages 58-59)

## Internship-II (UC2001-1) - Societal + Research/Industry internship

- Duration: 8 weeks in total, done during the second and third years.
- When: in the breaks between the IV & V semesters and the VI & VII semesters, in one or two stretches.
- Structure: either
  - a mandatory 2-week societal internship (80-90 hours) plus a 6-week Research or Industry internship (240-270 hours), or
  - a Research or Industry internship of 8 weeks in total (320-360 hours).
- It may involve innovation, entrepreneurship, or short-term (about 2 weeks) society-related activities plus a 6-week industry internship.
- Evaluated in the VIII semester: CIE 50 marks + SEE 50 marks (100 marks), 8 credits.

(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, VIII semester scheme table and sections 3.6 and 11.5(iii), PDF pages 30, 33 and 59)

## Where can Internship-II be done?

Students may work on innovation or entrepreneurial activities (possibly leading to a start-up), or intern with an
industry, NGO, government organisation, or micro/small/medium enterprise (MSME). Innovation and entrepreneurial
activities can be carried out at the institution's Incubation Centre and Entrepreneurship Cell, or elsewhere, for
example ATAL Incubation Centres (Atal Innovation Mission, NITI Aayog), institutes of national importance, public
sector units, IT companies, government organisations and NGOs. Institutes discourage internships at places set
up only for commercial benefit. AICTE internship opportunities are listed at https://internship.aicte-india.org/.
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, sections 3.6 and 3.6.6-3.6.7, PDF pages 59-62)

## Diary, report and assessment

Once the internship starts, students must keep a diary/journal and regularly submit a report to their guide
summarising the previous week's activities. At the end of the internship, each student submits a hard copy of the
consolidated diary/journal and a report showing what they learnt and achieved. The evidence is the diary plus the
internship or activity report, with a certificate or declaration from the relevant authority. The work is assessed
by the institute faculty mentor together with an external expert, and graded Excellent (80-100), Good (60-79),
Satisfactory (40-59) or Unsatisfactory/fail (below 40).
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, section 3.6.8 and Table B3, PDF page 62)

## Benefits of industry internships

Industry internships expose students to a real workplace and give them practical experience. They also provide
chances to attend seminars and workshops and build contacts with professionals, publish papers, get
recommendation letters for higher studies and placements, develop teamwork and leadership skills, and improve the
chance of a placement offer from the same company.
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, section 3.6.7, PDF pages 61-62)

## Societal internship

A societal internship is a short internship of about 2 weeks in villages, slums or urban areas, preferably done in
teams. Teams can work on agriculture, watershed management, wasteland development, non-conventional energy, low-cost
housing, sanitation, nutrition and personal hygiene, skill development, income generation, blood banks, or government
schemes such as Swachh Bharat, Accessible India (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan), Digital India and Beti Bachao Beti Padhao.
Societal activities (environment and energy conservation, legal aid, consumer protection, the Indian Red Cross
Society, NCC, Bharat Scouts and Guides, etc.) support the NBA graduate attributes PO6 (The engineer
and society) and PO7 (Environment and sustainability). In rural areas this work can take the form of a rural
internship. In urban areas, students may adopt slum or economically weaker areas for a short social internship.
Internship coordinators are asked to encourage students to take up a societal internship wherever possible.
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, section 3.6.5, PDF pages 60-61)

## Major Project and advanced internships

In the VIII semester, students do the Major Project Phase II (UC3001-1, 8 credits) in a research institute,
industry or an intra-institute Centre of Excellence, with two contact hours per week with the project guide.
Students can also choose an advanced internship or research internship in an industry, research institution or
centre of excellence. A regular student must have earned at least 122 credits before submitting the project
report (88 for lateral entry students).
(Source: 2023 B.Tech Regulations, sections 8.4-8.5 and 14.4-14.5)

## Older B.E. scheme (admitted 2021-22, 160-credit regulations)

Under the 2024-25 B.E. Regulations, Skill Courses (Project Work / Internship / Seminar) carry 28-36 credits, and
the assessment of seminars, industrial internship and field work follows the rules laid down by the Academic Council.
(Source: 2024-25 B.E. Regulations, sections 5 and 8.6)
